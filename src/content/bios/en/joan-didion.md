As a teenager, Joan Didion typed out Hemingway's books to see how his sentences worked. Born in Sacramento in 1934, she won an essay contest run by *Vogue* in her final year at Berkeley and went on to work there for seven years.

She was a journalist and essayist first. *Slouching Towards Bethlehem* (1968) collected her magazine pieces about California, including writing on the hippie counterculture of San Francisco's Haight-Ashbury. It is often cited as a New Journalism book: real events told with a novelist's tools, by a reporter who puts herself in the story. She also wrote novels, such as *Play It as It Lays* (1970), set in Hollywood, and screenplays with her husband, the writer John Gregory Dunne.

At the end of 2003, their daughter Quintana was in a coma when Dunne died suddenly of a heart attack. Didion wrote about the following year in *The Year of Magical Thinking*, which won the National Book Award for Nonfiction in 2005 and which she later turned into a Broadway play. Quintana died in 2005, while her mother was on tour promoting the book. Didion wrote about her in *Blue Nights* (2011).

Start with an essay. In *The White Album* (1979), the title piece goes back to the summer of 1968, when Didion herself was not well. Then read *The Year of Magical Thinking*. Joan Didion died in New York in 2021.
