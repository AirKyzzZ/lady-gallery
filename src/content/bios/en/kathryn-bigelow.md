Kathryn Bigelow was a painter first. She studied at the San Francisco Art Institute in the early 1970s and was accepted into the Whitney Museum's Independent Study Program in New York, where Susan Sontag was one of her advisers. She then went to film school at Columbia, where she made *The Set-Up*, a 20-minute short that takes apart violence on screen.

That subject stayed with her. She took on genres seen as male territory: vampires in *Near Dark* (1987), a cop thriller in *Blue Steel*, then *Point Break* (1991), with Keanu Reeves as an FBI agent going undercover among bank-robbing surfers. It became her most profitable studio film.

*The Hurt Locker* follows a bomb disposal squad in the Iraq War, shot in a near-documentary style. In 2010 it made her the first woman to win the Academy Award for Best Director. Many in the audience noticed that her acceptance speech did not mention it. She went on to make *Zero Dark Thirty* (2012), *Detroit* (2017) and *A House of Dynamite* (2025).

Watch *Point Break*, then *The Hurt Locker*. Going from a surf heist film to a war film, you can see how she bends genre rules.
