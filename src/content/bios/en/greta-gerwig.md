Greta Gerwig wanted to be a playwright. When playwriting MFA programmes turned her down, she switched to acting. While still a student at Barnard College in New York, she started appearing in tiny independent films in 2006, part of the scene later labelled "mumblecore". She paid the rent as a nanny and an SAT tutor.

Working with Noah Baumbach changed things. The two co-wrote *Frances Ha*, in which she plays the title role, and it earned her a Golden Globe nomination.

In 2017 she directed her first solo feature, *Lady Bird*, which she has called a love letter to her hometown of Sacramento. Saoirse Ronan plays the lead, and went on to star in Gerwig's *Little Women* (2019). Both films deal with girlhood, artistic ambition and mothers and daughters. *Lady Bird* brought Gerwig an Oscar nomination for Best Director.

Then came *Barbie* (2023), co-written with Baumbach. It passed a billion dollars worldwide, the first film with a woman as sole director to do so. In 2023 Netflix hired her to write and direct two films based on *The Chronicles of Narnia*.

Start with *Lady Bird*, then rewatch *Barbie* with her shoestring beginnings in mind.
