Cindy Sherman works alone in her studio, acting as photographer, director, make-up artist, hairstylist, wardrobe department and model. She appears in almost all of her pictures, yet she insists they are not self-portraits. "Sometimes I disappear," she has said.

She went to Buffalo State to study painting, found it limiting and switched to photography. She was already dressing up in thrift-store outfits, and a fellow student, Robert Longo, encouraged her to photograph the process. Between 1977 and 1980, living in Manhattan, she made the *Untitled Film Stills*: small black-and-white prints in which she plays women from film noir and B-movies, a librarian, a seductress, a hillbilly. None has a title, so the story stays open. The Museum of Modern Art bought the series in 1995.

Colour and larger formats came next. *Centerfolds* (1981) twists the magazine centre spread. In *History Portraits* (1988 to 1990) she restages European portrait paintings. Later she turned herself into clowns, and then into wealthy older women for the *Society Portraits* (2008). These days she also posts heavily edited selfies on Instagram.

French visitors have had a few chances to see her work in person: a touring retrospective stopped in Bordeaux in 1999, and the Fondation Louis Vuitton in Paris gave her a major show in 2020. Start with *Untitled Film Still #21*, shot out on the streets of New York and picked by Time as one of the hundred most influential photographs. It looks like a scene from a film you have seen. That film does not exist.
