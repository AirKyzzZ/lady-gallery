Katharine Hepburn grew up in Hartford, Connecticut. Her mother headed the Connecticut Woman Suffrage Association, and as a child Hepburn went along to "Votes for Women" marches. She started acting at Bryn Mawr College, got good reviews on Broadway and was brought to Hollywood. Her third film, *Morning Glory* (1933), won her the Best Actress Oscar.

Then things went wrong. After *Bringing Up Baby* (1938) flopped at the box office, a group of cinema owners put her on a list of stars they called "box office poison". She bought out her contract with RKO, left Hollywood and starred on Broadway in Philip Barry's *The Philadelphia Story*. She got hold of the film rights and sold them on one condition: she would play the lead. The 1940 film was a hit and put her back on top.

She went on to make nine films with Spencer Tracy, then found a new line in mature, independent women, as in *The African Queen*. Three more Oscars followed, including one for *The Lion in Winter*, a record for the category. She never turned up to collect them. In 1969, past 60, she played Coco Chanel in the Broadway musical *Coco*.

Start with *Bringing Up Baby*, a commercial failure that critics loved, then watch *The Philadelphia Story*, the film she made happen by buying the rights herself.
